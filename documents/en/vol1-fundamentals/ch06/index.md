# Classes and Object-Oriented Programming

Classes are C++'s mechanism for bundling "data" and "operations" together, and they are the cornerstone of object-oriented programming. In this chapter we start from the simplest class definition and gradually work out how to write constructors, when destructors get called, how `static` members differ from ordinary members, and what friends—this "targeted grant of access"—are all about. Once you understand these, you will be able to write your own well-encapsulated types.
